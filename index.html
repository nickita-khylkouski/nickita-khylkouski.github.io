How I got here

I started coding on Replit in fifth grade. In ninth grade, my Java class used it too. I helped classmates until I became the unofficial second teacher. Years later, I won the Alif x Replit hackathon, shown here in a photo from that period, and ended up working with people whose editor I had been using for years.

Hackathons followed the same loop. First I entered them. Then I started winning. Then I helped run them and judge them. I learned that I liked the systems around a project almost as much as the project itself: who gets in, how teams form, and which signals actually matter.

Peak Security

Peak Security is where I work on AI-assisted recon, exploit validation, CVSS triage, evidence packaging, and responsible-disclosure workflows. The blunt version is that I find the trust boundary, prove the impact, and help fix it.

Peak made $40K profit in its first month. It was also a YC Summer Fellow and a Palantir Valley Forge Fellow. Those fellowships were for Peak, not separate side quests.

I have earned $50K+ from security research and responsible disclosure across startups, enterprise systems, AI infrastructure, and public-sector systems. I keep the public version short. The work is usually less glamorous than the headline and more useful than it sounds.

Replit

At Replit, I help build abuse and moderation workflows, then test user Repls and platform surfaces for vulnerabilities. It is a good full-circle job for someone who started coding there in fifth grade.

People, events, and ranking systems

At Cerebral Valley and AI Valley, I built admissions, event operations, mobile app, messaging, judging, and recruiting-intelligence systems. The work touched 6,000+ enriched builder profiles, 5,000+ hackathon users, 19 AI events, and 3,700+ attendees.

I got interested in the problem of ranking people without flattening them. Cerebral Valley chose roughly 500 people from more than 20,000 applicants for one event. The useful part was not the number. It was building a system that could find good builders from messy evidence instead of rewarding whoever had the cleanest resume. That work shows up in Metric.

Metric ranks builders by demonstrated work. It has ranked 30,000+ people and predicted two of the top three hackathon winners in one backtest. I built it with my brother. The photo here is from that chapter.

Catan Zero

Catan Zero is a reinforcement-learning research stack for a strong two-player, no-trade Settlers agent. I built it with Michael Burton, who holds the top Catan Elo ranking. We met through YC fellows after he demoed a model trained on scraped Catan games.

The project mixes expert iteration, search, native simulation, and multi-GPU training. It is also a good example of how I like working: start with a game, find the hard part, and go down the rabbit hole.

Other things I care about

I taught AI fundamentals to 60 students and reached 2,500+ people through teaching and outreach. I have competed in USACO through Platinum and reached number one globally on Exonic for a period. I lift, run, and still take photos of graffiti when I see a good wall.
